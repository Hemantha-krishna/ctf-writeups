## Why CySA+?

Before taking CySA+, I had already earned several cybersecurity certifications, including **CompTIA Security+, TCM Security's PSAA and PIPA, and TCM Security's PJPT and PNPT**.

I wanted to continue building on both my foundational knowledge and hands-on experience.

CySA+ was a good opportunity to deepen my understanding of **security operations, threat detection, vulnerability management, incident response, and security analysis**.

Rather than being my first step beyond Security+, CySA+ became another milestone in a broader journey of developing both the theoretical knowledge and practical skills needed for a career in cybersecurity.

One of the things I appreciated about the certification was that it wasn't simply about memorizing definitions. You need to understand what you're looking at and, more importantly, what you should do with that information.

That made the preparation more challenging, but also more valuable.

## Practice Exams: Don't Be Discouraged by Low Scores

For practice exams, I used **Jason Dion's CySA+ practice tests**.

My scores were generally in the **65–75% range**.

Seeing those scores wasn't particularly encouraging at first, but looking back, I think it was actually helpful.

The practice questions felt somewhat tougher than what I encountered on the actual exam. That forced me to spend more time understanding *why* an answer was correct instead of simply trying to memorize the answer.

For me, the goal wasn't:

> "How can I get a high score on this practice test?"

It was:

> "Why did I get this question wrong, what concept am I missing, and am I making the same mistake elsewhere?"

That distinction made a big difference.

---

## How I Used AI to Study My Wrong Answers

One thing I did differently during my preparation was use **AI as a study partner**, particularly when reviewing questions I got wrong.

Instead of simply looking at the correct answer and moving on, I would take the questions I missed and use AI to help me understand **why I got them wrong**.

I would ask AI to:

- Explain the concept behind the question in simpler terms
- Explain why my selected answer was wrong
- Explain why the correct answer was the best choice
- Look for **patterns in the types of questions I consistently got wrong**
- Identify concepts I repeatedly misunderstood
- Help me distinguish between similar technologies or security concepts

This was useful because an individual wrong answer doesn't always tell you much.

But when you start looking at **multiple wrong answers together**, patterns begin to emerge.

For example, I could recognize that I wasn't simply struggling with one particular question. I might have been consistently misunderstanding a specific concept or confusing two similar technologies.

Instead of continuing to review the entire CySA+ curriculum equally, I could focus my time on the areas where my mistakes were actually concentrated.

For me, AI wasn't a replacement for the courses, practice exams, or hands-on labs. I used it as an **additional layer of analysis** to help me understand my mistakes and make my study sessions more targeted.

The goal wasn't to memorize the answers.

It was to understand the pattern behind the mistakes so I wouldn't make the same mistake when faced with a different question.

## PBQs: Where Hands-On Experience Helped

One part of the CySA+ exam that I found particularly interesting was the **performance-based questions (PBQs)**.

I believe my previous hands-on experience from earning the **Practical SOC Analyst Associate (PSAA)** certification from [**TCM Security**](https://www.tcm-sec.com/) helped me quite a bit here.

The practical experience gave me exposure to scenarios that required me to think through problems rather than simply recall definitions.

This is one of the biggest lessons I took away from the exam:

**Hands-on experience matters.**

You can read about logs, alerts, vulnerabilities, network traffic, and incident response all day, but actually working through security scenarios gives you a different level of understanding.

If you're preparing for CySA+, I would strongly recommend incorporating some hands-on labs into your study routine.

You don't need to spend every hour studying for the certification exam itself. Building practical skills alongside your exam preparation can make the concepts much easier to understand.

---

## Read Every Answer Choice: Look for the "Best" Answer

Another important lesson from the CySA+ exam is that **more than one answer choice can sometimes appear to be correct**.

This is where carefully reading the entire question—and every answer choice—becomes extremely important.

You may see multiple options that could technically address the problem. However, the question may ask you to choose the:

- **Best** answer
- **Most appropriate** answer
- **Most effective** answer
- **Most likely** explanation
- **Most impactful** action
- **First** or **next** step
- **Most appropriate response**

Those words matter.

Don't stop reading as soon as you find an answer that seems correct.

**Read every option before making your selection.**

The question isn't always asking:

> "Which of these could work?"

It may be asking:

> "Which option is the *best* choice given this specific situation?"

That's an important distinction.

I found that it helped to slow down enough to identify exactly what the question was asking before evaluating the choices. Then I would compare the options against the specific scenario rather than choosing the first answer that sounded technically correct.

This is especially important for scenario-based questions, where several answers may be reasonable but one may be more appropriate based on the circumstances described.

**Read the question carefully. Read every answer. Pay attention to the wording. Then choose the answer that best matches what the question is actually asking.**

---

## My Biggest Exam Tip: Skip the PBQs at the Beginning

If there's one exam strategy I'd recommend, it's this:

**Skip the PBQs at the beginning and come back to them later.**

The reason is simple: PBQs can take a significant amount of time.

I didn't want to spend too much time on one difficult scenario while knowing there were other questions I could answer much more quickly.

Instead, I focused on getting through the multiple-choice questions first, answering the ones I knew, and flagging questions that required more thought.

Then I returned to the PBQs toward the end.

Of course, everyone has a different exam strategy, so do what works for you. But if you're worried about running out of time, consider saving the PBQs for later.

**Time management is just as important as knowing the material.**

## Final Thoughts

Passing CySA+ with an **858/900** was a great milestone, but I'm even more interested in what I learned during the process.

The biggest takeaway for me was that certification preparation becomes much more valuable when you combine **theory, practice questions, AI-assisted analysis, and hands-on experience**.

If you're currently studying for CySA+, don't be discouraged by lower practice-test scores.

Use them as feedback.

Figure out what you don't understand, look for patterns in your mistakes, go back to the material, and keep practicing.

And when you sit for the exam, remember that knowing the material is only part of the challenge.

**Managing your time, understanding what the question is actually asking, and approaching the choices strategically matter too.**

I'm excited to build on this foundation and continue developing my cybersecurity skills.

If you're on the same certification journey, I hope this helps—and good luck with your CySA+ preparation! 🚀
